# Pakistan Post-Harvest Losses: Mango and Rice

This document summarizes official and field-research statistics on how much of Pakistan's mango and rice harvest is lost after harvesting, and where in the supply chain those losses occur.

## The Scale of the Problem

Pakistan's own National Commission on Agriculture has estimated that defects in post-harvest handling, transport, storage, and marketing cause **20-40% losses** for fruits and vegetables generally — a figure echoed across multiple more recent industry sources. For horticultural crops specifically, losses in the 30-40% range are widely cited by Pakistan's Horticulture Development and Export Company (PHDEC) and related industry analysis, meaning roughly a third of what farmers grow may never reach a paying customer in sellable condition.

## Mango: Where the Losses Happen

Mango losses in Pakistan are concentrated in the **farm-to-mandi transport stage**, not at the farm or at the final retail point. A 2026 market analysis specifically named Multan and Mirpurkhas — two of Pakistan's major mango-growing areas — as examples of regions where fruit traveling in open (non-refrigerated) trucks through the intense May-July heat arrives at the wholesale market already discounted in value, simply because of the journey itself. This matches the broader industry consensus that Pakistan's lack of cold-chain infrastructure between farm and market, rather than problems at the farm level, is the primary driver of mango losses.

Academic research on the mango value chain similarly points to poor harvesting technique, improper cold storage, and lack of proper transportation as the three compounding causes — problems that span the entire chain rather than any single weak link.

## Rice: Where the Losses Happen

Rice losses in Pakistan tell a different story than mango, since rice is far less perishable and the loss points are more spread out across the value chain.

Pakistan's own Trade Development Authority (TDAP) reported that while rice post-harvest losses in most developing countries run 14-16%, **Pakistan's losses can reach 20-30%**, attributed specifically to the continued use of traditional (rather than mechanized) cultivation and harvesting methods. Within that total, TDAP's analysis (drawing on a six-country FAO study) breaks losses down by stage:

- **Storage**: roughly 4-6% of total post-harvest loss, worsened in Pakistan by inadequate storage capacity.
- **Drying**: 1-5% loss, mainly affecting grain quality rather than quantity — a particular concern since 70-90% of Pakistan's farm-produced paddy is still sun-dried rather than mechanically dried.
- **Milling and storage combined**: a more recent (2019) field survey by WWF-Pakistan, conducted directly with farmers and mill operators in Peshawar, Islamabad, and Lahore, found quantitative losses as high as **30% at the mill stage**, with additional qualitative losses (moisture loss affecting grain quality) of 3-6% specifically during storage. This is the most directly-measured, Pakistan-specific rice loss figure available, as opposed to older secondary estimates.

## Why This Matters for Storage/Transport Decisions

The loss patterns for the two crops point to different intervention priorities:

- **For mango**, the biggest opportunity to prevent loss is in the transport leg — getting fruit off open trucks and into any form of temperature-controlled transport or minimizing time-in-transit during peak heat hours.
- **For rice**, the biggest opportunity is proper drying before storage and adequate storage capacity — since rice loss is driven more by moisture mismanagement over weeks/months than by a single hot truck ride.

This is consistent with the shelf-life data in the crop profiles: mango's exponential decay is fast and temperature-driven (matching the transport-loss finding), while rice's decay is slow and moisture-driven (matching the storage/drying-loss finding).
